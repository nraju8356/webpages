The Process

How a professional car paint job is done

Each stage builds on the one before it. Skipping or rushing any step shows up in the final finish — sometimes immediately, sometimes months later.

01

Inspection

The vehicle is assessed under bright workshop lighting. We document existing damage, note areas of rust or previous repair, and discuss your requirements. This determines the scope of work and helps us give you an accurate quote.

02

Surface Preparation

All trim, handles, and removable components are taken off. The existing paint is assessed for adhesion. Areas that need repair are identified and marked. Good preparation is what makes the difference between a finish that lasts and one that doesn't.

03

Sanding

The entire surface is sanded to create a uniform profile for the new paint to bond to. We use a combination of machine sanding for large areas and hand sanding for edges and contours. Dust extraction keeps the environment clean.

04

Priming

Epoxy or filler primer is applied to seal the surface and provide a stable base. Primer fills minor imperfections and ensures the colour coat adheres properly. It's sanded smooth once cured before the next stage.

05

Colour Matching

We identify your vehicle's paint code and mix the base coat to match. For older vehicles or colour changes, we use a spectrophotometer and test panels to get the match right before applying paint to your car.

06

Painting

The base coat is applied in a controlled paint booth with filtered air and consistent temperature. Multiple thin coats are built up to achieve full coverage and even colour. Flash-off times between coats are critical.

07

Clear Coat

A protective clear coat is applied over the colour. This provides UV protection, chemical resistance, and the depth of gloss you see on a finished vehicle. Two to three coats are typical, depending on the paint system.

08

Polishing

Once fully cured, the clear coat is wet-sanded and polished to remove any minor imperfections and bring up the final gloss level. This is where the finish goes from good to excellent.

09

Final Inspection

The vehicle is reassembled and inspected under multiple light sources. We check for colour consistency, surface quality, and proper fitment of all components before it's ready for collection.

Common Questions

About the painting process

How long does car painting take? +
A full respray typically takes 5 to 10 working days, depending on the vehicle size, condition, and paint system. Single panel repairs can often be completed in 2 to 3 days. Curing time is a significant portion of this — rushing it compromises durability.
What happens before painting? +
The vehicle is stripped of trim and components, sanded to bare metal or a stable existing finish, repaired where needed, primed, and sanded again. The paint booth is cleaned and the environment checked for temperature and humidity before any paint is applied.
How is the paint colour matched? +
We start with the manufacturer's paint code, which gives us the base formula. For accurate matching — especially on older vehicles where the original paint has faded — we use a spectrophotometer to read the actual colour and adjust the mix. Test panels are sprayed and compared under different lighting before we commit to the vehicle.
How many coats are applied? +
Typically 2 to 3 coats of primer, 2 to 4 coats of base colour (depending on the colour and coverage needed), and 2 to 3 coats of clear coat. The exact number depends on the paint system, colour, and desired finish.
How long should new automotive paint last? +
A properly applied professional paint job should last 10 to 15 years before showing significant fading or wear, assuming normal use and basic maintenance like regular washing. Harsh climates, poor storage, and lack of care will reduce this.

Full Respray

Complete vehicle repainting

A full respray is the most thorough option for restoring or changing a vehicle's exterior finish. It involves stripping, preparing, and repainting every exterior panel to a consistent standard.

When a full repaint makes sense

Full resprays are appropriate when the existing finish has widespread failure — peeling clear coat, extensive fading across multiple panels, or heavy scratching. They're also the right choice for colour changes, restoration projects, or vehicles where panel-by-panel repair would leave visible inconsistencies.

If damage is limited to one or two panels and the surrounding paint is in good condition, a targeted repair is usually more practical and cost-effective.

"The quality of a full respray is determined in the preparation stage. The actual painting is the final step in a long process."

01

Original Colour Assessment

We document the current finish, note any areas of concern, and confirm whether you're keeping the original colour or changing it. This affects the preparation approach.

02

Body Preparation

All exterior trim, lights, handles, and mouldings are removed. The vehicle is washed, decontaminated, and masked. Any dents or rust are repaired at this stage.

03

Primer Application

The entire vehicle receives a uniform primer coat. This seals the surface, fills minor imperfections, and provides a consistent base for the colour coat.

04

Base Coat

The colour is applied in multiple thin coats until full, even coverage is achieved. Metallic and pearl colours require careful technique to avoid mottling.

05

Clear Coat

Multiple layers of clear coat are applied for protection and gloss. This is what you see and touch on the finished vehicle.

06

Curing

The paint is allowed to cure fully, either at ambient temperature or in a controlled bake cycle. This is not optional — insufficient curing leads to premature failure.

07

Polishing

The cured clear coat is cut and polished to achieve the final gloss level and remove any minor surface imperfections from the painting process.

08

Reassembly & Final Check

All components are refitted, gaps are checked, and the vehicle is inspected under multiple light sources before release.

What Affects the Result

Factors that influence finish quality

The final appearance of a full respray depends on several factors beyond the painter's skill. Understanding these helps set realistic expectations.

Does the original body condition matter? +
Yes. Paint follows the surface beneath it. Dents, waves, and poor previous repairs will show through a new finish. Bodywork quality is as important as paint quality.
What paint systems do you use? +
We use professional-grade waterborne and solvent-based systems from established manufacturers. The specific system depends on the job requirements, colour, and environmental regulations.
How do I care for a new paint job? +
Avoid washing for the first 30 days. After that, use pH-neutral car shampoo, soft mitts, and dry with microfiber. Avoid automatic car washes with brushes. Regular waxing or ceramic coating will extend the life of the finish.

For vehicle owners in the local area, we offer drop-off and collection from our workshop. If you'd like to discuss a full respray for your vehicle, see our FAQ or get in touch for an assessment.

Paint Repair

Targeted repair for paint damage

Not every paint issue requires a full respray. Scratches, stone chips, and localised clear coat failure can often be repaired while preserving the surrounding factory finish.

Types of damage we repair

The most common paint issues we see are scratches from keys or shopping trolleys, stone chips on bonnets and bumpers, faded clear coat on horizontal surfaces, and peeling clear coat on older vehicles. Each requires a slightly different approach.

Scratches

From light surface marks to deeper scratches that reach the primer or metal.

Stone Chips

Small impact damage, typically on leading edges and horizontal surfaces.

Clear Coat Failure

Peeling or flaking of the protective clear layer, common on older vehicles.

01

Damage Inspection

We assess the depth and extent of the damage to determine the repair approach. Surface scratches may only need polishing; deeper damage requires filling and repainting.

02

Cleaning

The affected area is thoroughly cleaned and decontaminated. Any loose paint or rust is removed to create a stable surface for repair.

03

Sanding

The damaged area and surrounding paint are sanded to create a smooth transition. The feathered edge ensures the repair blends seamlessly.

04

Priming

If bare metal is exposed, primer is applied to prevent corrosion and provide a base for the colour coat.

05

Colour Matching

The existing paint colour is identified and matched. Even small repairs need accurate colour matching to avoid visible differences.

06

Spot Painting

Colour is applied to the repaired area only. The spray pattern is controlled to keep overspray to a minimum.

07

Blending

The new paint is blended into the surrounding area to avoid a hard edge. This may involve spraying a light coat over adjacent panels.

08

Clear Coat

Clear coat is applied over the repair and blended into the surrounding finish for consistent gloss and protection.

09

Polishing

The repaired area is polished to match the gloss level of the surrounding paint. The goal is a repair that's invisible at normal viewing distance.

Customer Questions

About paint repair

Can all scratches be repaired without repainting the whole panel? +
Most can. Light to moderate scratches that haven't reached bare metal can often be polished out or touched up. Deeper scratches may need localised repainting, but this is still less invasive than a full panel respray.
How long does a paint repair take? +
Most repairs are completed within 1 to 3 days, depending on the extent of damage and curing time required. Same-day service is sometimes possible for very minor repairs.
Will the repair be visible? +
A well-executed repair should be very difficult to spot at normal viewing distance. Up close, a trained eye might find it, but that's true of most professional repairs. The goal is invisible at arm's length.
Is paint repair covered by insurance? +
It depends on the cause of damage and your policy. Accident-related damage is typically covered; wear and tear or environmental damage usually isn't. We can help assess whether a claim is worthwhile.

Colour Matching

Accurate automotive colour matching

Getting the colour right is one of the most technically demanding parts of automotive painting. Modern paint systems use complex formulations, and even small variations are visible to the eye.

How colour matching works

Every vehicle has a paint code, usually found on a plate in the door jamb, under the bonnet, or in the boot. This code identifies the original colour formula. We use this as our starting point.

For vehicles where the original paint has faded or been previously repaired, we use a spectrophotometer to read the actual colour and adjust the mix accordingly. Test panels are sprayed and compared under different lighting conditions before we apply paint to your vehicle.

"Colour matching isn't just about the formula — it's about accounting for how the original paint has changed over time."

01

Paint Code Identification

We locate your vehicle's paint code and look up the manufacturer's formula. This gives us the base composition for the colour.

02

Paint Sample Comparison

We compare the formula against physical samples and your vehicle's actual colour. This reveals any variations due to age, fading, or previous repairs.

03

Colour Mixing

The paint is mixed to the adjusted formula. Metallic and pearl colours require precise control of flake orientation and concentration.

04

Test Panel

A small test panel is sprayed with the mixed colour. This is compared against your vehicle under controlled lighting.

05

Lighting Comparison

The test panel is checked under daylight, fluorescent, and incandescent lighting. Colours can look different under each source, and we need a match that works in all conditions.

06

Paint Application

Once the colour is confirmed, it's applied to your vehicle using the same technique and conditions as the test panel.

07

Final Blend

The repaired area is blended into surrounding panels to ensure a seamless transition. This is especially important for metallic and pearl colours.

Why Blending Matters

Understanding panel blending

When repairing a single panel, the new paint needs to blend into the adjacent panels. This is because even a perfect colour match can look different if the application technique or clear coat thickness varies. Blending involves spraying a light coat of colour over the neighbouring panels to create a smooth transition.

This is standard practice in professional auto body work and is necessary for an invisible repair. It doesn't mean we're repainting entire panels — just feathering the colour at the edges.

Why can't you just match the colour exactly? +
You can match the colour very closely, but the way paint looks also depends on application technique, clear coat thickness, and how the metallic flakes orient themselves. Blending accounts for these variables.
Does blending mean repainting other panels? +
Not fully. Blending involves spraying a light coat over adjacent panels at the edges only. The majority of those panels retain their original finish. It's a targeted technique, not a full respray.
How accurate is spectrophotometer matching? +
Very accurate for the sample being read. The challenge is that vehicle paint isn't uniform — different panels may have faded differently, and previous repairs may have used slightly different colours. The spectrophotometer gives us a precise reading; experience tells us how to apply it.

Cost & Pricing

Car painting cost factors

Pricing depends on the scope of work, vehicle size, paint system, and body condition. Here are the main factors that affect cost.

What determines the cost of a paint job? +
Vehicle size, extent of damage, paint type (solid, metallic, pearl), body preparation required, and whether it's a full respray or localised repair. Colour changes cost more than matching the original.
How much does a full respray cost? +
Full resprays typically range from £2,000 to £6,000+ depending on the factors above. We provide detailed quotes after inspection so there are no surprises.
Is a cheaper paint job worth it? +
It depends on your priorities. Budget paint jobs often cut corners on preparation or use lower-grade materials. The finish may look acceptable initially but fail prematurely. For a vehicle you plan to keep, investing in quality preparation pays off.
Timing

Painting time & scheduling

How long will my vehicle be in the workshop? +
Full resprays: 5–10 working days. Panel repairs: 2–3 days. Minor scratch repairs: sometimes same-day. These are working days — actual calendar time may be longer depending on scheduling.
Can I use my vehicle while it's being painted? +
No. The vehicle needs to remain in the workshop for the duration of the work. We'll agree on a collection date when the job is confirmed.
Preparation & Aftercare

Before and after painting

What should I do before bringing my vehicle in? +
Remove personal belongings from the vehicle. We'll strip exterior trim as part of the process, but interior items should be taken out. If the vehicle is particularly dirty, a basic wash helps with the initial assessment.
What should I check when collecting my vehicle? +
Inspect the finish under different lighting conditions. Check that all trim and components are properly refitted. Look for consistent colour across panels and a uniform gloss level. Any concerns should be raised before you leave.
How do I maintain a new paint job? +
Don't wash for 30 days. After that, use pH-neutral shampoo, soft mitts, and dry with microfiber. Avoid brush car washes. Apply wax or ceramic coating every 6–12 months. Park in shade or a garage when possible.
Insurance & Claims

Insurance-related repairs

Do you work with insurance companies? +
Yes. We work with most major insurers on approved repair claims. We can also provide independent assessments and quotes if you're managing the repair privately or through a third party.
Will insurance cover a full respray? +
Insurance typically covers damage from specific incidents (accidents, vandalism, weather events). It doesn't cover wear and tear, fading, or pre-existing damage. We'll advise on what's likely to be covered after assessment.
